REM ============================================================
REM Script 6 (extended): NAVIGATION + ALT-NUMPAD + SYSRQ
REM Purpose: covers keyboard command types not present in
REM scripts 1-5 - arrow/navigation keys, ALT+Numpad character
REM entry, and SysRq - now combined with a two-paragraph typed
REM body so this script's STRING timing is comparable to the
REM others too. All keyboard-only, no mouse.
REM Behavioural signature:
REM  - Arrow-key repeats fire at a perfectly constant interval
REM    (humans show acceleration/deceleration and occasional
REM    overshoot-correction when navigating with arrow keys).
REM  - ALTCHAR/ALTSTRING sequences are a strong tell on their
REM    own, since this input method is almost never used by a
REM    human typing normally.
REM  - SYSRQ commands are single-shot and essentially never
REM    appear in ordinary typing at all.
REM ============================================================
DELAY 500
GUI r
DELAY 300
STRINGLN notepad
DELAY 800

DEFAULT_STRING_DELAY 65
STRINGLN Keystroke dynamics research looks at the rhythm and timing of how people type rather than what they type. Every person presses and releases keys with a slightly different cadence, shaped by muscle memory, keyboard layout, and even mood. Automated input, by contrast, tends to move at a much steadier and more predictable pace. Capturing enough examples of both styles lets a model learn to tell one from the other.
STRINGLN This dataset is meant purely for defensive research and classroom experimentation. None of these scripts perform any destructive action, and every one of them only types benign sentences into a text editor. The goal is simply to generate clean, labeled timing signatures for building better detection tools. Over time, these signatures can help protect systems against unauthorized automated input without punishing real users for typing quickly.
